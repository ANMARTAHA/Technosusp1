Pharmaceutical Suspensions — condensed for revision
Pharmaceutical Technology · 3rd stage, 1st semester

Overview

key definitions & laws only

Definition

  • A suspension = finely divided drug particles (the suspensoid) distributed through a vehicle in which the drug has minimum solubility.
  • Supplied ready-to-use, or as a dry powder reconstituted before use (for drugs unstable in solution).

Why suspensions are used

  • Drug is chemically unstable in solution but stable when suspended.
  • Liquid dosage form is easier to swallow and dose is flexible.
  • Masks disagreeable taste (e.g. erythromycin estolate — a less soluble ester used for a palatable liquid form).

Desired features of a good suspension

  • Therapeutic efficacy, chemical stability, permanency, aesthetic appeal.
  • Settles slowly; redisperses readily on gentle shaking.
  • Particle size stays fairly constant on standing.
  • Pours readily and evenly.

Stokes' sedimentation equation

V = d²g(ρₛ − ρf) / 18η
  • V = sedimentation velocity, d = particle diameter, g = gravity.
  • ρₛ, ρf = density of particle & medium; η = viscosity of medium.
  • Sedimentation is significant for particle radii ≥ ~0.5 µm.
  • ↓ particle size by 10× → ↓ settling rate by 100× (d is squared).

Particle size reduction methods

  • Micropulverization — fast, cheap, ~10–50 µm; for most oral/topical suspensions.
  • Fluid energy grinding / jet milling — under 10 µm; used for parenteral or ophthalmic suspensions.
  • Spray drying — for extremely small particle dimensions.
  • Ideal particle diameter in most good suspensions: 1–50 µm.

Caking & particle shape

  • Over-reducing particle size favors compact "cake" formation that resists redispersion.
  • Symmetrical barrel-shaped particles (e.g. calcium carbonate) → more stable, no caking.
  • Asymmetrical needle-shaped particles → tenacious sediment cake that cannot be redispersed.

Flocculation

  • A floc/floccule = a loose aggregate of particles held by weak bonds — prevents rigid caking.
  • Flocs settle faster than fine individual particles but form a bulkier, redispersible sediment.

Sedimentation volume ratio

F = Vf / Vo
  • Vo = initial suspension volume; Vf = final sediment volume.
  • F ranges 0–1.
  • Flocculated system: F up to 0.6.
  • Deflocculated system: F as low as 0.1.

Flocculated vs deflocculated

  • Flocculated: settles fast (minutes–hours), loose/fluffy sediment, clear supernatant, easily redispersed.
  • Deflocculated: settles slowly (days–weeks), dense "caked" sediment, hard to redisperse.

Flocculating agents

  • Clays — diluted bentonite magma (oral suspensions); also supports the floc structure.
  • pH alteration toward minimum drug solubility (parenteral suspensions).
  • Electrolytes — reduce the electrical barrier between particles, bridging them.
  • Nonionic / ionic surfactants at controlled concentration.

Suspending / thickening agents

  • Added to lend structure to the dispersion medium when settling is too rapid.
  • Examples: carboxymethylcellulose (CMC), methylcellulose, microcrystalline cellulose, polyvinyl pyrrolidone, xanthan gum, bentonite.

Thixotropy

  • A highly flocculated preparation is semi-solid at rest but becomes fluid when shaken, and rebuilds structure on standing.
  • Ideal: fluid long enough to dose easily, then regains viscosity quickly.

Best sedimentation pattern — the trade-off

  • Two factors: speed of sedimentation and reversibility.
  • Deflocculated = slow settling but poor reversibility risk (irreversible coagulation if too viscous / over heated).
  • Flocculated = controlled slow settling with easy redispersion — usually the practical balance.

Preparation — wetting

  • Poorly wetted powders clump or float; must be wetted before dispersion.
  • Wetting agents: alcohol, glycerin, propylene glycol — displace air in crevices, aid penetration of the dispersion medium.
  • Large scale: colloid mill. Small scale: mortar and pestle.

Preparation — assembly & preservation

  • Dispersion medium (with soluble colorants, flavorants, preservatives already added) is blended into the wetted powder in portions.
  • Remaining vehicle rinses equipment and brings suspension to final volume.
  • Preservatives against bacteria/mold: methylparaben, propylparaben.
